01 The Problem

Travel apps are built for tourists. Nobody builds for the rest.

Remote work, long stays, and rolling relocations have created a huge new class of traveler — the in-between. They stay two weeks, two months, sometimes forever. They don't want to feel like tourists, and they don't have a tool built for them. That's our user.

The long-stay traveler

Two months in Lisbon. Cobbling together Reddit, Google Maps, and a friend's texts.

02 The Product

Six layers that turn a visitor into a believable local — and remember every city you've lived into.

№ 01

The Shell

Basics · the platform

One app to install. Account, passport of cities, points, streaks. Stays minimal worldwide. The civic loads on top.

S
№ 02

The Civic

Each city, curated

The Code (unwritten rules), the Map (places by vibe), the Rhythms (this week), the Quests (depth, not breadth), the Perks (real local discounts). One paid curator per city.

C
№ 03

The Persona

Vibe Check · then an AI that learns you

Starts simple: Vibe Check — pick your mood (quiet coffee, slow walk, night out, date night) and the map shows the places and neighborhoods that match. Every place card carries a vibe line in a local voice. Later, an AI learns your vibe over time and builds itineraries from it.

P
№ 04

The Blend

Wear · Speak · Gesture · Sound

Four things that mark you as someone who belongs: one piece of attire to add, ten phrases with native audio, local gestures — namaste, the four-way wave, the elevator silence — and the Sound, the track the city is playing this week. The visible-tourist tells, neutralized.

B
№ 05

The Mates

Certified locals · badged, not ranked

Real humans who pass a civic fluency exam, not a job interview. Badged by domain — Food, Nightlife, Outdoors, Old Soul. Meet for coffee. No tour script. The deepest version of the civic.

M
№ 06

The Passport

Journal · Wishlist · Cross-city memory

Your travels, threaded. Every city auto-stamps your moments. Wishlist things from TikTok — surfaced when you arrive. Eat Indian food in Portland? The app remembers when you land in India. The more cities, the smarter it gets.

Rule №07 · Etiquette
Locals don't use
umbrellas.
A hood, a hat, or nothing at all. Umbrellas mark you as new.
→ Why
Rain here is light and constant — not the kind that ruins your hair. An umbrella is a 30-second commitment for a 2-second sprinkle. Locals zip up and keep walking.
→ Instead, do this
Pack a waterproof shell. Wear a brimmed hat. Embrace the drizzle — you'll start to like it.
→ Exception
Sideways downpour in November. Then everyone caves.

04 The Persona

Your app gets to know you, then plans the city around it.

On install, an AI agent has a real conversation with you — your taste, your mood, your purpose for being here, your hobbies, what you avoid. It builds a profile that lives with you across every city. Then anywhere you go, you can ask anything in your own words — the agent already knows what you mean.

Think Gemini on Google Maps — but the agent has been listening to you across cities, and it answers like a friend who knows your taste, not a search engine. — The Persona Layer

05 The Passport

Your travels, threaded.

Every city you live into becomes a chapter you keep. Auto-stamped quests, Mate chats, places you returned to, dishes you loved — saved as a journal you'll re-read for years. Wishlist things from TikTok and they surface the moment you arrive somewhere they fit. Eat Indian food in Portland because someone said it was the best outside India? When you land in Bangalore, the app remembers.

"Most travel apps die when the trip ends." Basics doesn't. The longer you use it, the more your cities start talking to each other. That's the unfair advantage. — Why Passport is the moat
